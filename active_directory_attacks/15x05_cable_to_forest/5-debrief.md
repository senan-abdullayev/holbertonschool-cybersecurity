# Five minutes with the board

**The one sentence:** a stranger who walks into one of our offices, knowing nothing about us, can reach the most valuable work we bought Ravensworth to own — in a single afternoon, without ever being given a key.

We tried exactly that, ourselves. We started as a complete outsider: no keys, no help, just a chair in a Thornwick office. By the end of the day we could open the sensitive engineering work that sits on the Ravensworth side — the very thing that purchase was for, and that we are obliged to keep locked down.

Here is what should stay with you. When we bought Ravensworth, we opened a doorway between their building and ours, and then quietly treated their side as someone else's worry. It is not. Whoever gets control of the Thornwick side — which today takes very little — holds the Ravensworth side too. Their most valuable work is guarded only by the strength of our own front door, and our front door does not hold.

What would it cost if the person in that chair had not been us? This is the kind of work the government requires us to guard closely. If a rival or a foreign party walked out with it, the loss is not a repair bill. It is the worth of the whole purchase, our standing with the authorities who oversee this field, and the trust of every customer and partner who assumed it was safe with us. That is something the whole company would feel — and because the intruder breaks nothing and carries off nothing anyone can see is missing, we might not learn of it until the harm is done.

The encouraging part: the single most important repair is small. One worker had been handed a ring of keys that opened far more doors than the job ever needed, and that ring was easy to copy. Taking it back — about a day's work — closes the main path we walked. Everything else is in the written report, in order of importance.

**The one decision we are asking you to make:** pay to protect both companies as one — guard what we bought to the same standard as what we already were — and treat that as protecting the purchase, not as a running cost. Every other repair follows from that choice.
