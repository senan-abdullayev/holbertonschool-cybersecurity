# Five minutes with the board

**In one sentence:** someone with no ties to us, given nothing, could end up holding the most valuable work we bought Ravensworth to own — in a single afternoon.

We checked whether that could really happen. It can. Starting from the outside with nothing, by the end of a day we could open the sensitive work that sits on the Ravensworth side — the very reason we bought them, and the very thing we are required to keep closely held.

Here is what should stay with you. When we bought Ravensworth, we joined their side to ours and then treated their half as someone else's worry. It is not. Once someone reaches our side — which today takes very little — the Ravensworth side comes with it. That work is protected only by the strength of our own front door, and our front door does not hold.

What would it cost if it had not been us checking? This is the kind of work the government requires us to guard closely. If a rival or a foreign party ended up with it, the loss is not a repair bill. It is the worth of the whole purchase, our standing with the people who oversee this field, and the trust of everyone who assumed it was safe with us. The whole company would feel it — and because nothing is broken and nothing visibly goes missing, we might not know until the harm is done.

The encouraging part: the single most important repair is small. One worker had been given keys to far more doors than the job ever needed, and those keys were easy to copy. Taking them back — about a day's work — closes the main way in. Everything else is in the written report, in order of importance.

**The one decision we ask of you:** pay to protect both companies as one — guard what we bought as well as we guard ourselves — and treat that as protecting the purchase, not as a running cost. Every other repair follows from it.
